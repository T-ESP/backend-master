What is the AI Service?

A standalone microservice that analyzes your data and writes predictions.

It runs alongside the Rust API, sharing the same PostgreSQL database. The AI service writes predictions. The API reads them. They never communicate directly — the database is the integration layer.

01
Facebook Prophet

Demand Forecaster

Predict future product demand to prevent stockouts and optimize reorder timing.

How It’s Trained

1Pull up to 2 years of daily sales per product. Fill missing dates with zero.
2Fit a Prophet model with additive seasonality — automatically detects weekly and yearly patterns plus trend changepoints.
3Forecast the next 30 days with 80% confidence intervals (upper/lower bounds per day).
4Calculate recommended stock = sum(upper bounds) × 1.2 safety buffer. Derive reorder quantity and days until stockout.

02
Random Forest / Gradient Boosting

Price Suggester

Recommend optimal selling prices based on historical trends and market positioning.

How It’s Trained

1Build sliding-window training samples from price history. Uses prices[0:i] to predict prices[i].
2Engineer 8 features per sample: current price, mean, std, min, max, trend, volatility, position in range.
3Train both Random Forest (depth=10) and Gradient Boosting (depth=5). 5-fold cross-validation picks the winner by R² score.
4Predict optimal price per product. Safety cap: 50–150% of current price.

8 Engineered Features

current_price mean_price std_price min_price max_price price_trend volatility price_position

Confidence Scoring

confidence = 0.4 × data_confidence + 0.6 × model_R²

Only suggestions with confidence ≥ 0.7 generate alerts. Price changes are described in human-readable reasons: “Consider increasing price by 5.2% based on market trends.”

03
Isolation Forest

Price Anomaly

Flags products whose current price deviates from their expected price — catching pricing errors, data entry mistakes, or unusual market shifts.

Feature Engineering

price buying_price z_score margin_ratio

Per-product rolling average (30 entries) as baseline. Each product gets its own expected price, not a global average.

How Isolation Forest Works

Randomly partitions data using decision trees. Anomalies are easier to isolate (fewer splits needed), so they naturally get higher scores. The model expects ~10% of records to be anomalous (contamination = 0.1).

06
K-Means Clustering

Product Clusterer

Discover hidden product segments by grouping similar products based on sales behavior, margins, and trends.

How It’s Trained

1Engineer 6 normalized features per product (all scaled 0–1): revenue, variability, trend, seasonality, frequency, margin.
2Test K = 2 to 10 clusters. For each K, compute the silhouette score (how well-separated clusters are). Pick the best K.
3Run final K-Means. Each product assigned to its nearest centroid.
4Auto-name each cluster based on its centroid characteristics.

6 Features

revenue_score variability_score trend_score seasonality_score frequency_score margin_score
